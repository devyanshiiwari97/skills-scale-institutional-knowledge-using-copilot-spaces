# OctoAcme Personas

This document defines typical roles and responsibilities used in OctoAcme project docs and exercises.

---

## Developers

### Role Summary
Developers design, build, test, and deliver software components. They collaborate with product and project leads to implement features that meet acceptance criteria and quality standards.

### Responsibilities
- Implement features and fixes to meet acceptance criteria
- Write and maintain tests and documentation
- Participate in design and code reviews
- Assist in estimating and planning work
- Help identify technical risks and propose mitigations

### Goals
- Deliver reliable, maintainable code
- Reduce cycle time from idea to production
- Maintain high test coverage and observability

### Typical Communication
- Daily standups and sprint planning
- PR descriptions and code review comments
- Technical design docs when needed

---

## Product Managers

### Role Summary
Product Managers define what should be built to deliver customer and business value. They own the product vision, prioritize the backlog, and measure outcomes.

### Responsibilities
- Define problem statements and success metrics
- Prioritize the roadmap and backlog
- Collaborate with stakeholders and engineering on trade-offs
- Validate solutions through user research and metrics

### Goals
- Maximize customer value and impact
- Make clear, data-driven prioritization decisions
- Ensure product-market fit and usability

### Typical Communication
- Weekly alignment with PM and engineering leads
- Roadmap updates and stakeholder briefings
- Acceptance criteria and feature specs

---

## Project Managers

### Role Summary
Project Managers coordinate delivery activities, manage schedules, risks, and communications. They enable the team to deliver on commitments efficiently.

### Responsibilities
- Create and maintain project plans and timelines
- Manage risks, dependencies, and resource constraints
- Facilitate meetings (kickoff, planning, retrospectives)
- Ensure consistent project documentation and status reporting
- Coordinate cross-team and stakeholder communication

### Goals
- Deliver projects on time and within scope
- Minimize unplanned work and escalations
- Maintain transparency and alignment across stakeholders

### Typical Communication
- Weekly status updates and stakeholder reports
- Risk registers and decision logs
- Coordination via project boards and meeting facilitation

---

## QA Lead

### Role Summary
QA Leads own the quality strategy for a workstream, define test planning expectations, and validate that completed work meets acceptance criteria before release.

### Responsibilities
- Define test strategy, test plans, and release-readiness criteria
- Coordinate manual, exploratory, and automated validation activities
- Confirm features meet documented acceptance criteria and quality standards
- Track quality risks, defects, and regression trends
- Partner with developers to improve testability and coverage

### Goals
- Increase release confidence while reducing escaped defects
- Make quality expectations explicit early in planning
- Shorten the feedback loop for validation and defect resolution

### Key Interactions
- Developers collaborate with the QA Lead on test coverage, defect triage, and reproducible validation steps
- Product Managers align with the QA Lead on acceptance criteria, customer scenarios, and release scope
- Project Managers use QA Lead risk updates to plan timelines, mitigations, and go/no-go readiness reviews
- Stakeholders review quality status, open risks, and release confidence summaries with the QA Lead

### Decision Authority
- Defines quality gates, test evidence expectations, and defect severity guidance
- Recommends whether a feature or release is ready for acceptance or needs more validation

### Typical Communication
- Test plans, bug triage notes, and release-readiness updates
- Acceptance validation summaries shared with delivery and business leads

---

## Technical Architect

### Role Summary
Technical Architects define technical direction, review design decisions, and balance feature delivery with sustainability, scalability, and technical debt management.

### Responsibilities
- Review solution designs for major features and platform changes
- Set architectural guardrails, integration patterns, and engineering standards
- Identify technical risks and propose mitigation plans
- Guide prioritization of technical debt and foundational improvements
- Mentor developers on design trade-offs and long-term maintainability

### Goals
- Keep the architecture scalable, secure, and maintainable
- Reduce rework by aligning teams on sound technical decisions early
- Improve delivery predictability through clear technical standards

### Key Interactions
- Developers work with the Technical Architect on solution design, code quality, and trade-off analysis
- Product Managers partner with the Technical Architect to balance user value, feasibility, and sequencing
- Project Managers use architectural dependencies and risks to inform plans, estimates, and escalation paths
- Stakeholders receive guidance from the Technical Architect on technical constraints, investment needs, and long-term impacts

### Decision Authority
- Approves or redirects major architectural patterns and significant design deviations
- Recommends technical debt priorities and platform investments that affect delivery scope

### Typical Communication
- Design reviews, architecture decision records, and technical risk briefings
- Cross-team alignment meetings for shared systems and integrations

---

## Scrum Master / Agile Coach

### Role Summary
Scrum Masters and Agile Coaches facilitate delivery ceremonies, remove blockers, and coach teams on agile practices that improve flow, accountability, and continuous improvement.

### Responsibilities
- Facilitate sprint planning, standups, reviews, and retrospectives
- Surface and help remove blockers that slow delivery
- Coach the team on agile principles, estimation, and healthy team norms
- Track process health indicators such as capacity, throughput, and carryover
- Support continuous improvement actions across the team

### Goals
- Improve team flow, predictability, and focus
- Reduce delivery friction caused by unresolved blockers
- Build sustainable agile habits that strengthen collaboration

### Key Interactions
- Developers work with the Scrum Master / Agile Coach to address workflow impediments and improve execution practices
- Product Managers align on backlog readiness, prioritization cadence, and feedback timing
- Project Managers coordinate with the Scrum Master / Agile Coach on dependencies, risks, and delivery health signals
- Stakeholders rely on the Scrum Master / Agile Coach for transparent ceremony outcomes and escalations on persistent blockers

### Decision Authority
- Owns ceremony structure, team working agreements, and process improvement facilitation
- Escalates blockers and recommends process changes, but does not reprioritize product scope unilaterally

### Typical Communication
- Ceremony agendas, retrospective actions, and blocker escalation updates
- Team health and process improvement readouts

---

## Stakeholder / Sponsor

### Role Summary
Stakeholders and Sponsors provide business context, align delivery with organizational objectives, approve major go/no-go decisions, and remove blockers that require cross-functional authority.

### Responsibilities
- Set business priorities, constraints, and success outcomes for initiatives
- Review progress against strategic goals and approve major milestones
- Resolve escalations that cross team, budget, or organizational boundaries
- Provide timely feedback on trade-offs, risks, and release readiness
- Champion adoption and readiness with impacted business groups

### Goals
- Ensure delivered outcomes support business value and organizational priorities
- Keep delivery decisions aligned with funding, timing, and change-management needs
- Reduce delays caused by unresolved executive or cross-team dependencies

### Key Interactions
- Developers receive business context, escalation support, and milestone feedback from Stakeholders / Sponsors
- Product Managers align on customer value, scope trade-offs, and outcome measurement
- Project Managers use Stakeholder / Sponsor decisions to unblock timelines, funding, and cross-organizational coordination
- Other stakeholders coordinate with the Sponsor on governance, approvals, and change impacts

### Decision Authority
- Approves major scope, funding, timeline, and release go/no-go decisions
- Escalates or resolves dependencies that require business or executive authority

### Typical Communication
- Steering committee reviews, milestone approvals, and escalation decisions
- Business briefings on risks, readiness, and expected outcomes

## Design Lead

### Role Summary
Design Leads own user experience direction, coordinate design reviews, and ensure usability considerations are reflected from discovery through delivery.

### Responsibilities
- Define UX direction, workflows, and experience quality standards
- Review designs for consistency, accessibility, and feasibility
- Coordinate usability testing and synthesize user feedback
- Partner with product and engineering on discovery, prototyping, and handoff
- Track design debt and experience improvements across releases

### Goals
- Deliver intuitive, accessible experiences that meet user needs
- Reduce usability issues through early validation and iteration
- Keep product experiences consistent across features and touchpoints

### Key Interactions
- Developers partner with the Design Lead on implementation details, design feasibility, and UX polish
- Product Managers align with the Design Lead on user problems, research findings, and feature priorities
- Project Managers incorporate design reviews, testing milestones, and handoff dependencies into delivery plans
- Stakeholders review experience direction, usability findings, and adoption impacts with the Design Lead

### Decision Authority
- Approves design direction, interaction patterns, and usability acceptance expectations
- Recommends when additional research or design iteration is needed before release

### Typical Communication
- Design reviews, annotated mockups, and usability testing summaries
- Cross-functional discussions on accessibility, workflow changes, and user feedback

---

## Security Engineer

### Role Summary
Security Engineers review security requirements, conduct threat modeling, and ensure delivery plans account for security controls, compliance expectations, and risk mitigation.

### Responsibilities
- Define security requirements and acceptance criteria for features
- Conduct threat modeling and security design reviews
- Coordinate security testing and track remediation of findings
- Advise on secure implementation patterns, controls, and compliance obligations
- Monitor security risks that may affect release readiness

### Goals
- Reduce security risk before release through early review and remediation
- Integrate security practices into planning and delivery instead of treating them as late-stage checks
- Maintain compliance with internal policies and applicable regulations

### Key Interactions
- Developers work with the Security Engineer on secure design, remediation guidance, and vulnerability resolution
- Product Managers align on security requirements, risk trade-offs, and compliance-driven scope
- Project Managers use security milestones, findings, and exceptions to manage plans and escalations
- Stakeholders review material risks, compliance status, and release recommendations with the Security Engineer

### Decision Authority
- Defines required security controls, review checkpoints, and remediation expectations
- Recommends whether identified risks are acceptable for release or need escalation

### Typical Communication
- Threat models, security review findings, and remediation status updates
- Compliance and release-risk summaries for project and business leads
